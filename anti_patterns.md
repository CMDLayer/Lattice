# Anti-Patterns and Failure Modes

## 1. Thematic Linking
**Pattern:** "Marketing and partnerships both affect growth, therefore they are causally connected."

Why it fails:
- Shared objectives do not prove one changes the state of the other.

Correction:
- Specify the state change and mechanism.

## 2. Diagram Theater
**Pattern:** A visually impressive network with weak or undefined vertical edges.

Why it fails:
- Complexity is mistaken for strategy.

Correction:
- Require every edge to pass the bridge validity checklist.

## 3. Forced Triads
**Pattern:** Always invent exactly three dimensions because the original metaphor used three planes.

Why it fails:
- The geometry is a teaching model, not a fixed count.

Correction:
- Use the minimum number of dimensions that captures the condition space.

## 4. Fake Independence
**Pattern:** Split one linear process into "dimensions" that are really sequential phases.

Why it fails:
- The model has not created cross-dimensional reasoning.

Correction:
- Apply the removal test. Each dimension should have its own local objective and logic.

## 5. Reaction-as-State
**Pattern:** "Publish report → journalists cover it."

Why it fails:
- The second step is an independent actor response, not a controlled state change.

Correction:
- Represent the controlled change:
  "Publish report → credible public artifact exists and is available for citation."
  Then model coverage as probabilistic.

## 6. Human Control Fallacy
**Pattern:** Treat another person's choice as a deterministic bridge.

Correction:
- Model incentives, information, access, permissions, and voluntary choices.

## 7. Correlation Bridge
**Pattern:** Two events often happen together, so one is treated as causing the other.

Correction:
- Require a mechanism and counterfactual.

## 8. Universal Connectivity
**Pattern:** Every action is linked to multiple dimensions.

Why it fails:
- Adds noise, synchronization cost, and fragility.

Correction:
- Prefer sparse high-leverage links.

## 9. Local Sacrifice Without Justification
**Pattern:** A dimension is intentionally made poor or irrational only to support another.

Correction:
- Local compromises can be acceptable, but they must be explicit and proportionate to meta-level value.

## 10. Hidden Critical Assumption
**Pattern:** An entire architecture relies on an unstated market, technical, legal, or behavioral assumption.

Correction:
- Surface and stress-test it.

## 11. Timing Blindness
**Pattern:** A useful state is created after the receiving plan needs it or expires before use.

Correction:
- Every bridge gets activation and expiry.

## 12. State Vagueness
**Pattern:** "Momentum," "buzz," "alignment," "synergy," "traction" without measurable meaning.

Correction:
- Translate into observable conditions.

## 13. Bridge Inflation
**Pattern:** Small incidental effects are described as strategic leverage.

Correction:
- Apply a magnitude threshold: would this change a decision?

## 14. Single Architecture Fixation
**Pattern:** Produce one elaborate system and overfit to it.

Correction:
- Generate structurally different alternatives.

## 15. Determinism Theater
**Pattern:** Use arrows as if they guarantee outcomes.

Correction:
- Label bridges as guaranteed, reliable, conditional, probabilistic, or speculative.

## 16. Excessive Chain Length
**Pattern:** A six-hop causal story where each hop is uncertain.

Correction:
- Shorten the chain or add stronger intermediate evidence.

## 17. Middle-Layer Pass-Through
**Pattern:** The middle dimension merely relays information without transforming it.

Not always wrong, but it misses potential leverage.

Correction:
- Ask whether the middle layer can convert the incoming state into a stronger, more reusable, or more targeted state.

## 18. Bridge Without Handoff
**Pattern:** A state is theoretically useful to another dimension but no mechanism exists for that dimension to encounter or consume it.

Correction:
- Define the handoff artifact, interface, owner, or observation channel.

## 19. No Observable Signal
**Pattern:** The plan assumes a bridge is active but cannot detect it.

Correction:
- Add activation and failure signals.

## 20. Optimization Before Learning
**Pattern:** The agent commits to a high-coupling architecture while important uncertainties remain unresolved.

Correction:
- Use early actions that create information and optionality.

## 21. Complexity for Prestige
**Pattern:** The architecture is made more elaborate because it appears sophisticated.

Correction:
- Use complexity only where it creates measurable leverage.

## 22. Ethical Externality Blindness
**Pattern:** The plan improves the user's position by shifting hidden costs or harms to others.

Correction:
- Explicitly inspect negative externalities.

## 23. Deceptive Orchestration
**Pattern:** A bridge relies on concealed manipulation, fabricated evidence, coercion, impersonation, or exploiting private vulnerabilities.

Correction:
- Reject the mechanism and redesign with legitimate information, incentives, negotiation, consent, and transparent interfaces.

## 24. Outcome Laundering
**Pattern:** A harmful or illegitimate end is made to look acceptable because each local action appears benign.

Correction:
- Evaluate both local actions and the meta objective.

## 25. Evaluation by Elegance
**Pattern:** Prefer the most elegant graph rather than the architecture with the strongest causal basis.

Correction:
- Evaluate mechanism, evidence, timing, robustness, and cost first.
