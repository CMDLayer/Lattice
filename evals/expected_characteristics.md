# Expected Characteristics of Strong Outputs

A strong CDCO output usually has the following shape.

## 1. It starts from conditions, not favorite strategies
The architecture visibly reflects actual constraints and assets.

## 2. Dimensions are plausible even in isolation
No dimension exists solely to feed another.

## 3. Actions produce inspectable state changes
The output does not leap directly from one action to another dimension's desired behavior.

## 4. Bridges change decision-relevant conditions
The reader can explain what is easier, harder, faster, cheaper, safer, better informed, or newly possible.

## 5. Causal claims are calibrated
Some bridges are strong, some conditional, some optional.

## 6. The middle plane often transforms
In good three-stage chains, the middle dimension frequently converts one type of state into another.

Example:
`evidence -> partnership access -> distribution leverage`

## 7. The architecture is sparse
A few important vertical edges stand out.

## 8. Alternatives differ structurally
One architecture may optimize speed, another resilience, another learning/optionality.

## 9. Stress tests target bridges, not generic risk lists
The output asks what happens if a state is delayed, weaker, missing, or unusable.

## 10. First moves are chosen for multiple forms of value
Strong first actions often:
- advance the local plan,
- generate information,
- preserve options,
- and create reusable states for other dimensions.
