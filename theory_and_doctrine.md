# Theory and Doctrine

## 1. Working theory

Cross-Dimensional Causal Orchestration rests on five claims.

### Claim 1 — Actions create more than direct outputs
Every meaningful action changes a local state, but many actions also alter the wider condition space: information becomes available, uncertainty falls, access opens, resources move, timing changes, evidence accumulates, incentives shift, constraints appear or disappear, risk is redistributed, or optionality increases.

### Claim 2 — Effects can become inputs elsewhere
An outcome in one line of action does not have to be treated as an endpoint. It may become an input to another line of action. This creates a **causal handoff**.

### Claim 3 — Plans can be coordinated through state changes rather than commands
Two plans need not share actions, actors, or direct dependencies in order to be orchestrated. They can remain locally coherent while one deliberately changes conditions encountered by another.

### Claim 4 — Orchestration does not require deterministic control
A bridge is useful when it materially changes the downstream state space. It may:
- enable,
- constrain,
- accelerate,
- delay,
- de-risk,
- increase or decrease probability,
- change cost,
- reveal information,
- create access,
- improve evidence,
- alter incentives,
- or create options.

The downstream response may still remain uncertain.

### Claim 5 — Higher-order strategy exists in the relationships between plans
A system of plans can have additional structure that is invisible when each plan is observed independently. The orchestration is therefore not reducible to any single horizontal plan.

## 2. Horizontal vs. vertical reasoning

### Horizontal reasoning
Asks:
- What is the objective of this dimension?
- What should happen next?
- What prerequisites are required?
- What local sequence best advances the plan?

### Vertical reasoning
Asks:
- What state does this action leave behind?
- Which other dimension could use that state?
- What desired state on another dimension could this dimension create?
- Can a local action be timed or shaped to produce a stronger external consequence?
- Does a downstream plan transform that consequence into something useful to a third dimension?

CDCO requires both. Horizontal reasoning gives local coherence. Vertical reasoning creates orchestration.

## 3. The causal bridge

A causal bridge is represented as:

`Source action → state change → receiving condition → downstream affordance`

Example:

`Run pilot → usage evidence → financing dimension sees reduced uncertainty → stronger basis for due diligence`

The bridge is not:

`Run pilot → investor gives money`

The latter improperly assumes control over an independent actor. The framework should represent the actual change in the environment, not a guaranteed human response.

## 4. The causal handoff

A handoff occurs when a state change created by one dimension is intentionally made usable by another.

A handoff may be:
- **Direct:** a resource, artifact, or permission moves.
- **Environmental:** the receiving dimension encounters a changed environment.
- **Informational:** new evidence or information becomes available.
- **Temporal:** a timing window is created or synchronized.
- **Structural:** a constraint or capability changes.
- **Probabilistic:** the odds or expected cost of a downstream action change.

## 5. Independence principle

A dimension should not exist merely to make the diagram interesting.

**Independent coherence test:**
> If all cross-dimensional bridges were removed, would this dimension still have a legitimate objective and internally rational plan?

A dimension may become less valuable without the rest of the orchestration, but it should not become nonsensical.

## 6. Causal validity principle

A bridge is valid only if the source state change materially alters the receiving dimension.

**Counterfactual test:**
> If the source action or state change did not occur, would the receiving plan face a meaningfully different condition, choice set, cost, timing, information state, risk profile, or probability distribution?

If not, the relationship is probably thematic rather than causal.

## 7. Sparse leverage principle

Cross-dimensional planning becomes weaker when everything is linked to everything.

Prefer:
- fewer bridges,
- stronger mechanisms,
- clearer handoffs,
- lower coordination burden,
- and more reusable state changes.

A good orchestration often has a sparse graph with high-leverage edges.

## 8. Optionality principle

A bridge need not force one downstream action. Sometimes the best state change creates several viable future moves.

Optionality is especially valuable when:
- uncertainty is high,
- timing is unclear,
- actors are independent,
- or irreversible commitments are risky.

## 9. Transformation principle

The most valuable middle dimensions often do more than transmit an effect. They **transform** it.

Example:

```text
Product pilot
  ↓ creates usage evidence
Partnership program
  ↓ converts evidence into distribution credibility and access
Market expansion
  ↓ uses access to reduce entry cost
```

The middle plane is not merely a wire. It performs a causal transformation.

## 10. Recursion principle

An orchestration can itself be treated as a dimension inside a larger architecture.

For example:
- A product-launch orchestration can be one dimension inside a corporate growth orchestration.
- A hiring orchestration can be one dimension inside an organizational capability orchestration.

This allows nested planning, but recursion should be used sparingly to avoid complexity for its own sake.

## 11. Uncertainty doctrine

The agent must distinguish:
- **Guaranteed:** directly controlled and mechanically produced.
- **Highly reliable:** strong causal mechanism with low variance.
- **Conditional:** depends on known prerequisites.
- **Probabilistic:** changes likelihood but not certainty.
- **Speculative:** weakly supported and should not anchor the architecture.

Cross-dimensional orchestration should rely primarily on guaranteed, reliable, and conditional bridges, with probabilistic bridges explicitly labeled. Speculative bridges may be kept as options but not treated as load-bearing.

## 12. Human agency doctrine

When humans are involved, represent changes to their environment, information, incentives, permissions, or available choices without assuming or engineering involuntary behavior.

The skill should reject bridge designs based on:
- deception,
- coercion,
- blackmail,
- exploitation of private vulnerabilities,
- non-consensual manipulation,
- or intentional denial of meaningful choice.

Ethical orchestration can still involve persuasion, negotiation, signaling, reputation, incentives, and communication when these respect agency and applicable rules.

## 13. Minimal theory statement

> Complex plans can be coordinated not only through shared actions or direct dependencies, but also by deliberately shaping the conditions that other plans encounter. Because effects in one dimension can become inputs in another, strategic planning can operate both horizontally within plans and vertically across the causal relationships between them.
